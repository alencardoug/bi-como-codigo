## 7. Where I hesitated

Decisions that could have gone the other way, with the reason they went this one.

1. **The writer binds through a JSON Lines file** (exporter §16, choice 4) instead of §4.3's
   per-value parameters: `executemany` measured 49 s for M59's largest view. The file is clear data,
   inside the run directory only. Check that the cast path cannot infer a type, and that a value
   cannot change between the frozen reference and the table (the round trip should catch any).
2. **`export collisions` re-runs the read** (choice 5), because a run with an unapproved collision
   is refused by `verify` and leaves no result to read. It prints only hits in the committed public
   bytes — check that nothing else reaches the terminal.
3. **Extra connection parameters** (choice 1): `sslcert`/`sslkey`/`passfile` to an absent path,
   `gssencmode=disable`, `client_encoding=UTF8`, `connect_timeout=10`.
4. **`export catalogue` checks against the capture**, not a contract (choice 6), and measures the
   role closure.
5. **The invented `infinity` sentinel** in the adapter (choice 7) instead of letting psycopg's
   `DataError` refuse the fetch as `statement_failed`.
6. **Positive controls stop after the checked payloads** (`run.export(diagnose=…)`), not through
   sealing and the candidate, to keep `make check` near 5 minutes; the honest run and the candidate
   tests take the full path. Check that no row-check positive control needs the sealing to mean
   what it says.
7. **`site_fixture`'s integers have five digits** after one equalled 2026, a year
   `site/releases.md` cites. `invent` derives everything from the contract's types; a future type
   would need a branch.
8. **`release/views.json`'s headroom of 2×**, approved by the Owner; the plan said "with headroom"
   without a number.
9. **Two equal accepted builds are refused by the loader**, so "a row matching two accepted
   entries" (§12) is proved on the check alone with a hand-built contract.
