## 1. What changes, and what it reaches

- **Both sources change.** The legacy source is generated by the same engine and configuration as
  the main one — `legacy/cli.py` builds `Motor(carregar_config(), seed=catalogo.semente, …)` —, with
  its own seed and factor. A price or category fix therefore reaches `legacy_db` as well.
- **The stock ledger changes, so step 6 of §3.2 is not conditional here.** `unit_cost` is one of
  the business columns of the event (`streaming/sink.py`, `COLUNAS_DO_EVENTO`), and the generator
  takes it from the SKU cost (`generator/domains/estoque.py`, `__custo`), which the price fix moves.
- **A fix that keeps the number of random draws keeps every other column and key.** Each table has
  its own random source, seeded by its name (`tests/test_determinismo.py`,
  `test_dominios_sao_independentes_entre_si`). Whether any key moves is measured by stage 1.5 (H2),
  never assumed.
- **Nothing else depends on the category names or the single price range** — the question PLAN §9
  left to this stage (M25): zero matches outside the generator, in code, tests, dbt models, seeds
  and documents. The study trail has 24 matches, which N7 already covers.
- **What is destroyed, and where it survives:**

| State | What happens to it | Where it survives |
|---|---|---|
| CDC — topics, offsets, replication slot | Dropped (B1) and rebuilt by a new snapshot (C3) | Nowhere, by design ([Capacity §3.2](https://github.com/alencardoug/mvp_eng_dados_1/blob/1e197ac/docs/capacidade_e_recuperacao.md#32-o-cursor-de-cdc-não-está-no-pacote--e-por-quê)) |
| Both sources | Truncated and reloaded (B3, B4) | The pre-maintenance cut (A3) and, as of 2026-09-25, the approved package |
| `raw` and the hot sink | Reset and rebuilt from the new sources (B2, C2, C3) | Rebuilt, not kept |
| The SCD history — four snapshots | Dropped by `dbt-build RESET=1` (D3), rebuilt with one version. **Outside the survival claim of T3**: replaced by design | Only in the packages — the snapshots are unchanged since 2026-09-25 (M24); the cut's manifest keeps their oracle for a rollback |

## 4. The cycle

Every command runs from the eng_dados checkout, with the variables of P10. **Oracle** is the line
that counts — literal when quoted. **If it fails** is the restart point; §5 has the branches that
need more than a line, and §6 the way back. Each command goes to the diary with its UTC time, exit
code, oracle lines and deviation — "no", or what —, and its full output to `data/medicoes/v1_1_0/`,
named by line and attempt (S4).

**The capture ledger.** The diary keeps, from D2 on, every legacy capture attempt started after
the cut, with three states kept apart, because a failed invocation is not a failed capture: the
**invocation** — line, attempt, exit code —; the **Airbyte job** — its id and state, final only when
Airbyte says so (`airbyte.estado_do_job`, the question `captura.retomar` asks) —; and the
**certificate** — its `capture_attempt_id` and status in `governance.legacy_captures`. The id comes
from the output or the `JOB_EM` file, and when neither has it, from the attempt persisted in
`governance.legacy_captures`.

**One caller alive at a time** (N35). The claims of H6, H7 and H8 hold while a single capture
caller is alive — the CLI or a DAG run, not both, and not two of either. Two callers alive together
are outside them, a declared limitation (§7): the opening lock and the attempt lock are defence
beyond the claim, not a guarantee against concurrency, and T3 at D4 and F6 stays the independent
check of the memory. The cycle keeps the rule by construction — one operator, the window stopped,
a line at a time — and checks it: before each line that captures — D2, F3 and G7 —, `make
preflight ALVO=trabalho` gives "janela parada" and P8's check, which covers the CLI, exits 1,
both in the diary.

**The rule of the retained ledger: nothing captures, recovers or builds while a T4 record is due.**
An attempt is recorded as soon as its job is final, **with its certificate as it stands** — a
`pending` one recorded as `pending` (RR-21). Only the recovery phase finalises it later (H7):
`iniciar` no longer calls `retomar`, and `make sync-legacy` and the DAG both run the recovery phase
first, which appends the transition record the moment `retomar` makes the transition, before the
next attempt is opened, and stops on a recorded certificate changed in any other way (RR-28). Its
barrier then requires the whole inventory since the epoch's baseline — every attempt, `sync_id` and
job — recorded, and none `pending` (RR-32) —, run again inside `iniciar` under one lock as the opening
decision itself (RR-37), with an attempt lock between each caller's dispatch and recovery's
abandonment (RR-39); each caller records its own attempt at its end (H6); and a restore opens a
new epoch (H8, RR-34). T3(1) admits only the values of eng_dados' own
transitions (RR-27). After every line that built the
quarantine, whatever its outcome, T4 records the treatment before the next capture or build. In F3
the DAG captures and builds in one run, so the attempt record is the DAG's own gate (H6).

**When the attempt has no job id** — interrupted between `disparar` and `registrar_job`, in the
`airbyte` client or in the DAG's task, both of which create the job first —, its absence proves
nothing: the job may exist and have written rows (RR-24). The ledger reconciles three inventories:
the attempts of the certificate table, the `sync_id`s of `raw_legacy`, and Airbyte's job records of
the legacy connection (`airbyte.jobs`, which gives each job's `connectionId`, `jobType`, `status`
and `startTime`; M35). For an attempt with no id, its window runs from its `started_at` to the next
attempt's, or to the reading. Exactly one job of the legacy connection started in the window and
claimed by no other attempt is that attempt's job — and the attempt is recorded only once that job is
final, an active predecessor until then (H7) —: the ledger records the association, which the
certificate never receives — after `CARENCIA_SEM_JOB` the next `retomar` abandons the attempt, and
its rows stay retained, uncertified
([ADR-0044](https://github.com/alencardoug/mvp_eng_dados_1/blob/1e197ac/docs/adr/0044-certificar-cada-captura-do-legado-por-conteudo.md)).
No job in the window, and no `sync_id` in `raw_legacy` without an attempt, makes it an attempt with
no job. Anything else — two jobs, a job
or a `sync_id` no attempt explains, or a listing whose oldest job is newer than the window's start —
**stops for investigation before another capture or build**. Only jobs started after A8 are
reconciled: the earlier ones are A8's baseline (O8).

The ledger is T3's input. G7's restore capture goes in it too, though the restore's own oracle
validates it.

prediction for the changed data.

## 5. Stopping and starting again

The rules of B5 ([Stage 12 plan §7.4](https://github.com/alencardoug/mvp_eng_dados_1/blob/807a904/PLANO_etapa_12.md#74-pontos-de-parada-e-recuo)), with the differences the regeneration imposes:

- **A failing oracle stops the line.** The deviation goes to the diary; the cause is fixed at the
  source — document or code, with a commit —, and the line is redone. If a fix changes what an
  earlier line proved, the Owner decides from which phase the cycle restarts.
- **Once B1 has run, phase A is never redone:** A3 would cut the new state and call it the old one.
  The restart point is the earliest phase whose starting state still holds — B, since the sources
  regenerate deterministically; C, once B5 has passed; and after D2, the phase of the failed line,
  because every capture is memory.
- **E1 is taken once.** A restart inside phase E keeps E1's baseline; a second E1 would measure the
  producer's own events as the starting state.
- **No restart skips a due record.** Before a line is redone, every T4 record its failed attempt
  owes — attempt, transition, treatment — is taken, and an attempt whose job cannot be identified
  stops for investigation (§4). The transition a retry's own recovery phase makes, it records (H7).
  A failed DAG run is never continued by clearing or retrying its tasks: §5 first, then a new run
  (N31, F3).
- **A stop of the recovery phase is resolved, never bypassed** (H7). A predecessor still active is
  waited for, and the line run again. An attempt final with no record is recorded by hand with T4
  only with the window stopped — `make preflight ALVO=trabalho`, no DAG run or job under way, and
  P8's check exiting 1, since a gate still to publish is a caller in flight, not a lost record —, and only when nothing has captured, recovered or built since it
  ended — the diary says why. The preflight alone is no proof that a CLI has ended (RR-40): T4 takes
  the attempt lock without waiting and holds it through its publication, refuses when the lock is
  held — a caller still alive —, and refuses a first record of an attempt the epoch already holds. Otherwise it goes to the Owner. A transition whose record failed, or any other difference from the
  ledger, goes to the Owner, who decides whether its reading is recorded, marked as late, or the
  cycle goes back (§6) (N34).
- **A refusal of the preflight is not bypassed.** It goes to the diary; the Owner frees memory; the
  line is redone.
- **The cycle is not repeated without the Owner.**

### 5.1 The hot-path repair

When C4 or E10 fails, or C3 ends without its cut. The sink writes `on conflict (movement_id) do
nothing` ([sink](https://github.com/alencardoug/mvp_eng_dados_1/blob/1e197ac/src/mvp_ed1/streaming/sink.py)):
a wrong payload already written survives any replay, so the sink is emptied, not refilled.

1. P8's check exiting 1 — a surviving process goes through §5.2 first.
2. `make stream-down FORCE=1` — B1.
3. `make stream-reset-sink FORCE=1` — B2.
4. `make airbyte-up`; `make medir ALVO=sync-airbyte RESET=1` — the batch path rebuilt from the same
   source.
5. `make medir CENARIO=streaming` — a new snapshot, whose cut is the source's current
   `max(event_sequence)`.
6. The four zeros at that cut, with both paths at the source's current count.
7. After phase D: `make medir ALVO=dbt-rebuild`, then `cp dbt/target/run_results.json
   data/medicoes/v1_1_0/<line>_a<n>_rebuild_run_results.json`, and the 16 views of `consumption`
   counted. `dbt-rebuild` runs `--full-refresh` without dropping the snapshots — the restore's own
   target —: the incremental fact is rebuilt from the repaired paths, the SCD history stays, and the
   quarantine keeps every slice outside the current treatment — capture, catalogue version and
   fingerprint —, whose slice it recomputes. Then T4's treatment record.

The sources are not reloaded: B5 proved them, and the fault is downstream. After phase D, D3 is not
repeated either — it would drop the SCD history again. Nor is an incremental build trusted after a
repair: [its test](https://github.com/alencardoug/mvp_eng_dados_1/blob/1e197ac/dbt/tests/incremental_confere_com_a_reconstrucao_completa.sql)
compares the count, the quantity and the rounded cost, and an older event merged wrong before the
repair, with an unchanged sum, would stay stale outside the lateness window. If the repair fails a
second time, the Owner decides.

### 5.2 A pipeline that does not stop

When E8 leaves a `mvp_ed1.streaming`, Prism or Beam process behind — the orphan the meter was built
to avoid ([meter](https://github.com/alencardoug/mvp_eng_dados_1/blob/1e197ac/docker/medir.sh)).
The meter's own rule, by hand, on the process group E3 recorded and nothing else:

1. `kill -INT -- -<pgid>`, then P8's check once a second for up to 60 s — the meter's
   `MEDIR_PRAZO_ENCERRAMENTO`.
2. Still there: `kill -KILL -- -<pgid>`, recorded as a forced shutdown — the manual counterpart of
   the meter's `"encerramento": "forcado"`.
3. P8's check exiting 1 before the next line. A process outside that group goes to the Owner.

### 5.3 The producer stopped mid-way

When E4 fails or is interrupted. Each burst is committed before the cursor is saved, and the
cursor is written in place, not replaced atomically
([producer](https://github.com/alencardoug/mvp_eng_dados_1/blob/1e197ac/src/mvp_ed1/streaming/producer.py)):
the source can be one burst ahead of the cursor, and the cursor itself can be left truncated. A new
invocation reseeds from the cursor. If no burst of the stopped invocation was saved, it replays that
invocation's draws and collides on `movement_id`; otherwise it draws keys of its own and goes on
with the cursor still behind. Neither is a retry, and neither proves anything. So:

1. No new `stream-produce`; the pipeline stays up; `.stream/producer_state.json` is copied as it is
   to `data/medicoes/v1_1_0/` and never edited by hand.
2. **The cursor missing, truncated or unreadable:** stop. `_retomar` would read it as offset zero,
   and no O′ is invented. The copy is the evidence; the way back is §6, with the Owner.
3. Recorded: the cursor's `emitidos_acumulados` O′, the source's count C′ and its
   `max(event_sequence)`.
4. Reconciled against E1: Δcursor = O′ − O and Δsource = C′ − C0.
   - **Equal:** the events produced are consistent. The Owner decides between going on with
     N = Δsource from E5, and a new invocation for the remainder — recorded as a new run, with its
     own N, not as a retry.
   - **Δsource greater:** a burst committed after the last saved cursor. **Stop: the way back (§6),
     with the Owner.** The package must not carry a source and a cursor that disagree, and no tool
     repairs the pair: going forward would need a recovery operation of its own, reviewed, with an
     oracle that proves the repaired pair before the cycle resumes.
   - **Δsource smaller:** impossible by construction — the cursor is saved after the commit. Stop.

### 5.4 A promotion that fails

When G8 or G9 fails. `promover` removes the previous approved, then renames the candidate
([implementation](https://github.com/alencardoug/mvp_eng_dados_1/blob/1e197ac/src/mvp_ed1/recovery/pacote.py)),
so a failure leaves one of three states:

1. `ls data/recovery`, and the manifest of whatever is there.
2. **`candidato/` there:** the rename did not happen. `make recovery-verify` reads it (O2); if it
   verifies, `make recovery-promote` again — it removes what is left of `aprovado/` first.
3. **Only `aprovado/` there:** the rename happened. `make recovery-verify`, and its manifest's
   `commit` and cut are G3's, not v1.0.0's.
4. **Neither, or the one there does not verify:** never copy over it — `cp -a` merges, and a file
   left behind keeps the package invalid (M33). The invalid directory moves out of
   `data/recovery`, kept, to `~/mvp_ed1-recovery-invalid-<UTC>/`; G5's copy is verified —
   `sha256sum -c checksums.sha256` inside it, and its `manifesto.json` with G3's SHA-256 —;
   `test ! -e data/recovery/candidato`; `cp -a ~/mvp_ed1-recovery-v1.1.0/candidato data/recovery/`;
   `make recovery-verify`; and only then `make recovery-promote`. A failed verification stops: no
   package is merged or promoted on it.

The v1.0.0 packages are not at risk in any of the four: both live outside the checkout (G10).
